### What the references establish

**Papers, Please.** The official description assigns a precise role: an immigration inspector uses documents and inspection systems to decide entry, refusal, or arrest. The press kit identifies its pixel-art presentation. These facts support considering how a visible person, tangible object, and institutional role make an action feel situated.[^papers]

The assessment's proposed transfer is that sense of place and consequence. Ron's latest instruction rules out treating repeated document inspection as the selected activity for COALITION.

**Hundred Days.** In a pre-release gameplay note, Broken Arms Games describes the board as available time and cards as tasks. Cards offered at the beginning of a turn depend on what the player did in prior turns. Cards also carry restrictions and costs. Later release notes document task durations, employee work, and advancing turns.[^hundred-basics][^hundred-changelog]

The important design distinction is that a card can represent work made possible by the current state. The interface does not require every task to be a random event drawn from a shuffled deck. Applying that distinction to COALITION was an agent inference.

**Suzerain.** Torpor's description of the base game emphasizes decisions through conversations, characters with ambitions and ideologies, and effects on political and personal relationships. Reports, news, policies, and information about places help communicate the changing situation.[^suzerain]

The proposed contribution to COALITION is a cast whose cooperation has conditions and whose responses extend beyond the immediate exchange. The assessment did not import the additional war systems of the Rizia expansion or prescribe the scale of Suzerain's narrative.

**Balatro.** The official FAQ describes rounds with score requirements, restrictions imposed by Boss Blinds, Jokers that alter behavior or scoring, and cards that modify the deck. The developer and publisher's Steam description emphasizes combinations and choices that change a run.[^balatro-faq][^balatro-steam]

The proposed contribution is the development of a distinctive set of capabilities during an attempt. Poker hands, score targets, shops, and bosses were not recommended as requirements for COALITION.

| Reference | Agent's proposed use in COALITION |
| --- | --- |
| Hundred Days | Make work, time, and dependencies into understandable turns. |
| Balatro | Let acquired capabilities change how later situations can be solved. |
| Suzerain | Give NPCs continuing interests, relationships, and responses. |
| Papers, Please | Give decisions a visible place, person, and physical action. |

This mapping is a research-informed interpretation. It is not a finding that any particular combination has been proven to work.

### A possible player job

The assessment proposed that the player handle requests and commitments at one part of the journey. A dispatch office might involve assigning work and securing a departure. Another location might involve agreeing to terms or deciding which account to trust.

Possible actions included assigning, approving, deferring, asking, promising, reserving, and calling in help. Their common purpose would be to change what can happen next. Approving something could remain an action without requiring a document-discrepancy puzzle each time.

The proposed challenge was choosing where limited attention goes. A useful decision might pit an immediate arrangement against helping an ordinary person whose cooperation could matter later. The player would consider timing, reliability, competing requests, and the value of preserving options.

The agent proposed advancing the fictional clock when actions are committed. That would allow the player to read, think, or pause on a phone while departures, shifts, and offers change through turns. The exact duration of a turn, amount of available work, and form of the deadline remain undecided.

### The illustrative Varela level

**Authorship and status:** The agent invented Varela, the dispatch office, the crew problem, the party official, and the commitment card. These demonstrate a possible structure. They are not established Nelosphere lore or accepted COALITION content.

The scene is an evening transport office with waiting people and a service that is becoming difficult to keep running. The player acts as a local member of the opposition's support network. The immediate objective is to secure an onward journey for the target before the opportunity closes.

Before the target arrives, two requests compete for the available attention. Resolving a crew assignment helps an ordinary person and keeps a service available. Asking a party official to intervene may secure quicker help, but the official expects a commitment in return.

In the example, the player handles the crew request. On advancing the turn, the assignment is resolved and the scene changes. The person helped supplies a card representing a particular offer: “We can hold the departure.”

A later complication delays the target. The player can use the crew's offer to preserve the present opportunity, or allow it to close and pursue the official's alternative. The second choice carries an obligation. The first uses a specific promise that cannot be invoked indefinitely.

When the target arrives, earlier work determines what the player can actually arrange. The player commits to an available handoff, the departure occurs, and the next location opens. The outcome carries the chosen route, elapsed time, relationships, promises, and remaining help forward.

The example locates skill in reading a situation, sequencing work, recognizing the value of an ordinary request, and choosing when to use assistance. It does not establish a fixed turn count, required set of cards, or mandatory sequence for the game.

It also exposes a remaining question: if the player controls different local characters, what gives a commitment continuity across locations? It could belong to a person, a shared team, or the broader operation. The assessment did not select an answer.

### Turns, levels, and progression

The proposed structure distinguishes four scales:

| Scale | Proposed meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Turn | Respond to a situation, commit work or assistance, and advance time. |
| Level | Resolve a local problem with a recognizable objective and changing conditions. |
| Run | Attempt the escape, or attempt to prevent it, through connected situations. |
| Replay | Try again with different combinations of people, conditions, opportunities, and obligations. |

A level could be set at party headquarters, a transport office, a hotel, a communications room, or a terminal. Its purpose would be to make a necessary next step possible. These are possible locations, not an ordered campaign.

The agent proposed varying the main demand between levels. One could center on securing cooperation, another on timing several commitments, and another on interpreting conflicting information. A new setting would therefore change the decision as well as the scenery.

Some unsuccessful arrangements could lead to detours or a weaker bargaining position. Other failures could end the attempt. This was a proposed way to allow setbacks within a run; no failure rules were selected.

The service blueprint supplies the underlying questions: who waits for whom, what must happen first, who can act or authorize, what can happen at the same time, and what follows a refusal. Those relationships can be invented for the game. The player does not have to perform every background task described by the blueprint.

If both sides remain playable, their tasks can differ within the same situation. Facilitating could involve assembling cooperation and completing the handoff. Blocking could involve assessing information and choosing where to commit limited staff or authority. Both need meaningful limits on doing everything. The assessment did not design a second campaign or select balancing rules.

### Cards and a possible economy

The assessment proposed three card functions:

| Function | What it could represent |
| --- | --- |
| Situation | A request, arrival, complication, deadline, or changed condition. |
| Work | An action currently possible, including one enabled by earlier work. |
| Commitment | Help, access, an obligation, or an offer earned through an encounter. |

These functions do not imply three decks. The relationship between the card interface and random drawing remains open.

An established relationship could persist while particular offers become unavailable, expire, or are fulfilled. This would allow cards to express the state of a social relationship without requiring the person to vanish from play whenever their card is absent from a hand.

Ron originated the idea that people helped through competent encounters might provide cards useful later. The agent developed that into a possible economy of time, assistance, and obligations. A useful card might represent an introduction, access to someone's staff, or a specific remaining favor.

The unresolved incentive question is how the game distinguishes competent work from indiscriminate approval. If every assisted person provides an equally useful reward at no meaningful cost, choosing whom to help becomes less interesting. The fictional circumstances, scarce time, and different forms of assistance could supply the tradeoffs. No reward formula was chosen.

### Replay and the possible roguelike structure

The proposed connection to modern card roguelikes is a bounded attempt in which acquired capabilities affect later decisions. One run might depend on cooperation among ordinary workers. Another might depend on institutional support. Another might accumulate conflicting obligations to several people.

Variation could come from which requests appear together, who is available, what opportunities are closing, and what the opposing NPCs want. Each person's behavior would still need to make sense within the run. The blueprint can explain causes even when the player does not yet know them.

The assessment favored short recurring scenes that respond to prior decisions as a possible fit for replay. A character might respond differently because a promise was kept, help was exhausted, or the player arrived late. This is a proposal about narrative scope, not a measured production estimate.

In his own account of Balatro's solitaire influence, LocalThunk explains that unlocks and challenges encourage exploration and offer longer-term goals to interested players.[^solitaire] The COALITION assessment left such progression between runs open. Different circumstances and growing player understanding could be tested before choosing permanent unlocks or upgrades.

Having cards alone does not settle whether the game is a deckbuilder or a roguelike. A card interface, a shuffled deck, and the ability to change that deck are separate choices.

### What dice could do

Card draws can already introduce uncertainty. The assessment considered two additional roles for dice:

| Timing | Player decision | Potential effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roll before choosing | See the result and allocate the resulting capacity to work; use contacts or favors to change what is possible. | Creates a situation to adapt to while leaving choices after the roll. |
| Roll after committing | Choose a known risk and respond to the result. | Adds uncertainty to an outcome such as cooperation, refusal, or delay. |

The agent preferred testing visible choices first and giving dice a distinct purpose if included. A roll that determines capacity for work was one proposed option. Selected uncertain interactions were another.

The concern was repeated, unrelated luck checks that erase the value of preparation: requiring a particular draw, then a favorable roll, then avoiding another random cancellation. This is a design risk to test, not a finding that multiple sources of chance cannot work.

Dice remain an open part of Ron's concept. The recommendation to begin with card decisions did not authorize removing dice from the design. The computer game also has no settled requirement for a playable election phase or a dice-determined election result.

### Visual presentation and phone use

Ron specified an appealing pixel-art style. The assessment proposed detailed scenes in which actions visibly change the place: someone leaves their desk, a departure board updates, a waiting room fills, or a previously distant character approaches.

A possible screen would connect three things: the place and people, the work underway, and the available actions. Cards could appear as messages, request slips, calling cards, or other objects suited to the fiction. No layout or visual assets were produced.

Lucas Pope's account of adapting Papers, Please to phones is a useful primary reference. He describes portrait play, readable documents, and avoiding precision or zoom. He replaced the freely arranged desk with a large document carousel and navigation rack, while retaining a prominent face.[^papers-mobile]

The proposed lesson for COALITION is to design touch interaction around a clear current choice, with enough room for its person, terms, and consequences. A desktop screen could expose more context at once. The palette, typography, pixel scale, animation, and final control arrangement remain open.

### What a later prototype could examine

The assessment recommended one complete local situation with alternative ways through it and a later scene showing a consequence. This is a proposed evaluation, not an instruction to begin implementation.

The useful observations would be whether choosing an ordinary request is interesting, whether earned help changes the available actions, whether using help now has a meaningful cost, and whether earlier decisions change a later situation. Repeating the situation with different starting conditions could reveal whether the player forms a different plan.

The next brief still needs to select the player identity, ordinary actions, available information, role of recognition, card behavior, timing, and use of chance. Those decisions would define what a prototype is meant to demonstrate.
