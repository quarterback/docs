## Reading the record

The origin report remains the record of the earlier discussion. The checkpoint report records a later change of direction: the player would participate in the work surrounding the escaping figure, with a choice of allegiance. Earlier statements such as always playing the opposition figure should be read in their original context.

The reference-research AAR follows the checkpoint discussion. Ron asked how a level and progression could work while rejecting repeated Papers, Please document checking as the way through successive checkpoints. The AAR records the four-game research and an exploratory card-driven structure. Its Varela scene, local-coordination role, and card functions are assistant proposals. Saving the assessment does not establish their adoption as game rules.

The teaching report is a basis for developing an assignment. Its emphasis is the interpretation required before implementation: understanding the situation, mapping its dependencies, choosing a player perspective, and deciding what information and actions belong in the experience.

The game reports evaluate a developing concept. The blueprint makes the relationships and consequences of the imagined system available for design. The card economy, precise player role, ordinary activity, endpoint, and political scenario remain subjects for further design.

Ron explicitly clarified that the game can and should use entirely fictional details, including newly invented Nelosphere cities. The original article and existing world material provide inspiration; the game can invent or change its geography, politics, institutions, documents, and route.
